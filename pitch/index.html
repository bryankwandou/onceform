The problem

One form. Two people losing.

The person answering

Retypes the same twenty facts into every questionnaire they meet. Once submitted, there is no way to see where a value went, who holds it now, or to take it back. Consent is a checkbox nobody can audit.

The panel paying

Pays real money for responses, and roughly half are fraudulent — phone farms, AI-generated answers, one person wearing forty identities. Every defence available today means collecting more personal data.

Panels defend themselves by hoarding exactly the data that makes them worth attacking. The harder they try, the bigger the target.

The product

A vault, a claim, and a receipt.

1 · The vault

Seventeen fields, stored on the person's own device. What reaches the chain is one salted 32-byte commitment — enough to prove an answer came from this vault, useless for recovering any value in it.

2 · The claim

An issuer signs a statement about a wallet: distinct human, age bracket, country, employment. The panel checks the claim. The panel never receives the underlying fact.

3 · The receipt

Every disclosure writes a record the person owns: which fields, to which origin, when. They can withdraw it. The withdrawal is recorded too, so the history stays honest in both directions.

Steps one and two make the product usable. Step three is the one nobody has built.

The necessity test

Take the chain out and the receipt stops being evidence.

  • A receipt I could edit is worthless. If Onceform holds the consent record, then the company with the strongest motive to lose it is the one storing it.
  • The person must outlive the company. Receipts stay readable if this project is abandoned tomorrow. That is the whole promise, and a server cannot make it.
  • Panels verify without asking permission. No account, no API key, no rate limit. Screening is two public reads that keep working whether or not we like the panel doing them.
  • Issuers are not gatekept. Anyone can attest, exactly as anyone can write a reference letter. Who to trust stays the verifier's judgement rather than being hidden inside our contract.
